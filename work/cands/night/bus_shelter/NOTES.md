# bus_shelter — candidate notes

- Winner: **bus_shelter_c.js** (board-by-board carpentry), shipped as `game/assets/bus_shelter.js`. Final: 1,732 triangles, 116 meshes, measured 3.41 x 2.78 x 1.73 m (width includes the sign disc past the +X eave, height is the ridge cap), base at y = 0, centred, all four sides judged and none blank.
- Why C: it is the one that still looks built when you walk round it. Every plank is a real 0.18 m board with a 0.02 m gap over a dark core, on BOTH faces of all three walls, so the plank lines are there from outside and from the open front; the roof is five overlapping tile courses per slope over exposed cedar rafters with closed timber gables; the bench is a slab on two log rounds; the chrome-dark sign disc has a galvanised back plate and torus rim, which is what makes a dark disc read against a night sky; the lamp is a glowing tube in a dark trough under the ridge; dark sill logs give the style lock's dark line at the ground.
- Changed after seeing the renders: the first C had its boards on the inside face only, so the back and both side views showed a flat dark backing board (the gate passed it, the eye did not). The wall is now a dark core with boards on both faces. The gables were open and read as a hole in the side views, so triangular gable boards were added (an extruded triangle, one profile technique borrowed into C).
- A (primitives, 1,140 tris): timber panels with a dark groove box every 0.2 m on both faces. Clean and the groove lines read strongly, but a groove line on a flat panel is a decorated box, the plain dark disc disappears against a dark background, and the roof is two flat slabs. Thrown away.
- B (profiles, 852 tris): the walls are extruded weatherboard sawtooth profiles, the roof one chevron section with a chevron ridge cap, lathe disc and post, swept bench section. The idea is right, but an 8.5 degree board face under flat light is invisible: the back and sides render as plain brown walls. Thrown away; its gable boards and chevron cap were the best roof of the three.
- Deliberate deviations to flag: the sign post stands at x = 1.65 (just past the +X eave) so the 0.5 m disc clears the roof, which makes the width 3.41 against the 3.2 brief (6.6 percent, inside tolerance); the ridge cap puts the height at 2.78 (3 percent over). "Right end" was read as +X, which is screen-right in the front view. Log rounds and rafters use cedar bark (0x5a3f2c) next to the timber (0x7a5a3a) frame; both are named `timber`.
