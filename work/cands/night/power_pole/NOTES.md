# power_pole — candidate notes

- Winner: **power_pole_c.js** (loft column, braced arms), shipped as `game/assets/power_pole.js`. Final: 646 triangles, 30 meshes, measured 0.925 x 9.135 x 0.37 m, base at y = 0, centred, all four sides judged and none blank.
- Why C: it is the only one that has the Japanese pole silhouette rather than a generic one. Each cross-arm carries two diagonal galvanised struts back to the column, the way real arms are held, and those braces are what read at thirty metres. The column is one hand-built 10-point loft with a root flare and a raised joint band at 4.5 m (the two-piece splice on a real concrete pole), the transformer can hangs from a band clamp on the column by two hanger bars with a conical lid and two white bushings, the step bolts have hex nuts, and a dark open band marks the base (the style lock's dark line against the ground).
- Changed after seeing the renders: it first came in at 774 triangles against a 700 ceiling. The joint band went from four rings to three, and the brace struts and the insulator cone skirts became open-ended cylinders (both ends of every strut are buried in the arm and the column, the skirt's foot is on the arm and its top inside the sphere head) on DoubleSide materials, which took it to 646 with no visible change.
- A (primitives, 660 tris): a plain tapered CylinderGeometry column, box arms with clamp blocks, cylinder-and-sphere insulators, cylinder can with rims. Clean and in budget, but the arms are bare bars and it reads as any pole anywhere. Thrown away.
- B (profiles, 1,172 tris): lathe column and cap sleeve, C-channel extruded arms, five petticoat lathe insulators, a lathe can with a domed lid. The insulator profiles are the nicest of the three up close, but at 400 px the channel section and the petticoats are invisible and it is 67 percent over the ceiling. Thrown away.
- Deliberate deviations to flag: the brief puts three insulators on the 0.9 m arm; the middle of a symmetric arm is inside the column, so the top arm carries two and the third is a pin insulator on the column cap (the classic Japanese pole top), which keeps the five. The can hangs on +X (inside the arm span) rather than +Z so the depth stays at the can's own 0.37 m against the 0.4 m brief; the step bolts reach along +X and -X for the same reason. Height is 9.135 m because the cap insulator sits above the 9 m column top.
- Unresolved: the game strings the wires itself; the insulator positions are at x = -0.36 and +0.36 (8.44 m), x = -0.22 and +0.22 (7.64 m) and x = 0 (9.0 m) in the asset's own frame before recentring, which shifts x by about -0.01 m.
